# Design System: XRV750 Africa Twin, take it apart

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Crank Automaton"**

The page is a paper automaton pinned to a kraft-board wall. The bike is a cut-paper model standing on a kraft plinth, and the box under it carries a paper kickstart lever. The visitor yanks the lever down past its catch and lets go; the model jolts, the last separated piece flies off, and the next assembly lifts off the model on a folded kraft strut held by a brass paper fastener, showing the crimson card it was cut from at its edge. Crimson threads run from brass pins on the lifted piece to paper labels naming what it is. Every surface is a material that exists on a workbench: kraft board, pulp paper, slot tabs, brads, screws, masking tape, thread, a punched strip, a luggage tag. Nothing glows, nothing is glass, and there is no dark studio.

The first viewport is not a responsive layout but a measured composition: the approved comp (2752 x 1536) is reproduced inside a container, and every size in it is comp pixels times one unit, `--u = 100cqw / 2752`, so the whole scene scales as one object. Scrolling only moves the page; the machine is driven by hand. Below the strip-down the page becomes ordinary document flow, but the materials continue: paper sheets and tags taped or pinned to the same kraft wall, settling onto it as they arrive.

The imagery is AI-generated cut paper (Nano Banana Pro via kie.ai, backgrounds removed with Recraft). The strip-down's part layers are cut from the differences between neighbouring keyframes by the mask and split scripts in `../flight-lab`, and every shipped raster embeds its provenance, with a `.json` sidecar beside each webp. The build is Vite 8, React 19 and TypeScript, with GSAP timelines and one GSAP ticker for the strip-down, plain CSS and @fontsource faces.

Density is low and the interface recedes. Type is stencil letterpress for anything that names a thing and condensed workhorse sans for anything that operates or labels. Ink is a warm brown-black, never neutral grey, and every shadow is tinted umber.

**Key Characteristics:**
- One kraft ground (tiled `assets/plates/kraft.jpg`, 430px tile) under everything, with the kraft colour as its fill.
- A comp-unit hero: sizes are `calc(N * var(--u))`, positions are percentages of the comp frame from `layout.css`.
- A hand-driven machine: a kickstart lever with a catch, a jolt on every kick, pieces that fly off and lift on struts.
- Real paper devices: die-cut notches, slot tabs, brass brads, crimson thread, tape strips, punched perforations, tag holes.
- Crimson spent on the current step, its threads, the one live instruction and ink stamps; everything else is kraft, paper and brown ink.
- Soft, layered, umber shadows that read as paper lifted a few millimetres off board.

## Colors

A warm, low-saturation workshop palette of kraft and pulp paper in brown ink, with one saturated crimson and brass fittings.

### Primary
- **Rally Crimson** (crimson): sampled from the comp's active tab 01. The active rail tab, the current step number in the caption card, the lever's knob (in the plate), the crimson backing card at a lifted part's edge, the pointer threads and the edge of each pointer label, the kick arc and "Kick" label, the focus ring, text selection, and the Dakar year stamps.
- **Crimson Blush** (crimson-ink): text on crimson (the active tab, text selection).

### Tertiary
- **Brass** (brass): every paper fastener: brad pins on lifted parts, strut feet, thread pins, the pin on each name strip.
- **Brass Highlight / Brass Shade** (brass-light, brass-shade): the lit spot and the dark rim of the radial gradient on round brad heads (highlight, then brass at 45%, then shade).
- **Brass Rim / Brass Slot** (brass-rim, brass-slot): the outline of a flat brad drawn in SVG (strut feet, thread pins) and the screwdriver slot across it.

### Named Rules
**The Current-Step Crimson Rule.** Crimson marks what is happening now (the active tab, the step number, the part lifting off, the threads to its labels), the one thing to do now (the kick arc and label), or is ink stamped on paper (the Dakar years). It never fills a panel, a section or a decorative band.

**The Umber Shadow Rule.** Every shadow and cut-edge darkening is a warm umber at an alpha: Umber Shadow for paper off the board, Contact Umber where the model meets the shelf. No black, no grey, no coloured glows.

**The Brass Fitting Rule.** Anything that fastens is brass: a round brad head is the three-stop brass gradient, a flat brad in SVG is Brass with a Brass Rim and a Brass Slot. No other metal appears.

**The Accepted Headline Rule.** The hero title is Big Shoulders Stencil Display, slightly taller and narrower than the comp's AI-drawn stencil lettering, which has no free-font match. The user accepted this on 2026-09-27; it is an accepted deviation from the comp, not a match, and future work should not try to "correct" it toward the comp with tracking or weight hacks.

## Layout

**The first viewport** is `.comp-frame`: full width, aspect-ratio 2752/1536, a size container. On windows 900px and wider its width is capped at `100svh * 2752 / 1536` and centred, so the whole comp always fits the viewport. Regions (headline card, bike, plinth, spec chips, crank box and lever, rail, ten tabs) are absolutely placed by percentage variables measured from comp-2b in `src/styles/layout.css`; sizes inside them are comp pixels times `--u`. Left: the card (about 22% wide). Centre: the bike (about 43%) on the plinth, spec chips under the plinth's right half, the crank box below with the kickstart lever. Right: the rail board with a vertical "How it comes apart" label and ten stacked tabs.

**The strip-down does not pin.** The frame is one viewport in normal flow, and scrolling only moves the page. The step changes on a kick (to the next step, or from step 10 back to 0) or on a rail tab (to any step). The caption card replaces the intro card from step 1 on. Pieces, struts, threads and labels are drawn in the bike box's plate space (2360 x 1630), and pieces may swing past the frame's edge; `#root` clips horizontal overflow so they never widen the page. Pointer labels are slid back inside the window with an 8px margin.

**Below 900px** the comp restacks around the bike with `--u = 100vw / 1000`, inside one 100svh (min 600px) frame: the card full-width at the top (92vw, 43svh), the bike under it (96vw), a slimmer plinth, the crank box at the right under the bike with the "Kick" label on the wall to its left, and the ten tabs as a horizontally scrolling strip along the bottom that keeps the current tab in view. Pieces travel a shorter way (0.45 of their horizontal and 0.6 of their vertical path). At most two pointers show per step. Name strips, the plinth shadow, spec chips and the rail board are dropped; the card carries the part name.

**Below the strip-down**, sections are blocks of max 1280px, centred, with section and gutter spacing. Grids open at 720px (owner tags 2-up), 860px (spec table 2 columns, sheet head 2 columns), 960px (rally copy beside the Dakar strip; footer 3 columns) and 1100px (owner tags 4-up). Under 640px the Dakar strip turns vertical and is punched down both sides.

## Elevation & Depth

Depth is physical: paper a few millimetres off board. Almost every raised object carries a two-layer umber shadow, a tight contact shadow plus a wide soft one, and paper that is fixed to the wall (sheets, tags, labels) sits lower than paper that is moving (lifted parts). Cut and inset details use inset shadows (tab left edge, chip rim, tag hole). There are no hard offset shadows.

### Shadow Vocabulary
- **Card on board** (`box-shadow: 12u 18u 24u umber/0.36, 26u 40u 70u umber/0.3`): the headline/caption card.
- **Rail tab** (`inset 26u 0 18u -12u umber/0.45, inset 0 0 0 2u umber/0.3, 4u 6u 10u umber/0.3, 10u 16u 28u umber/0.18`): the tab emerging from behind the rail board.
- **Lifted part** (`filter: drop-shadow(14u 28u 20u umber/0.42)`): applied only once a part has left the wall.
- **Lever** (`filter: drop-shadow(4u 8u 8u umber/0.35)`): the kickstart lever over its box.
- **Name strip** (`0 2u 3u umber/0.3, 6u 12u 18u umber/0.28`): the paper strip naming the lifted part.
- **Pointer label** (`0 1u 2u umber/0.3, 4u 8u 14u umber/0.22`): the paper slip at the end of a thread; flatter than the name strip.
- **Tyre contact** (`radial-gradient(closest-side, contact/0.55, contact/0.25 55%, transparent)`): an ellipse under each tyre on the plinth, gone from step 4 on.
- **Sheet** (`box-shadow: 0 2px 3px umber/0.2, 8px 22px 44px umber/0.28`): the spec sheet below the fold.
- **Pinned paper** (`filter: drop-shadow(3px 8px 10px umber/0.3)`): owner tags; the Dakar strip uses a two-step drop-shadow of the same kind.

### Named Rules
**The Lift Earns the Shadow Rule.** A part at rest on the model has no shadow of its own; its drop shadow and brass pin appear only when it lifts (`data-lifted`), and the tyre contact shadows vanish from step 4 on, when the wheels are gone.

## Shapes

Paper silhouettes, not UI radii. The card and the spec sheet are rectangles with a die-cut V notch in the middle of each side (16u on the card, 14px on the sheet), made with clip-path. Rail tabs are square on the left (they come out from behind the rail) and rounded 12u on the right. Spec chips are square on top and rounded 14u below, hanging from the plinth. Owner tags are luggage tags: a clipped top with chamfered shoulders and a punched 14px hole. The Dakar strip is a band with 26px punched perforations along both edges, dashed tear lines between years, and a 1deg tilt. Name strips tilt 1.5deg away from their pin; pointer labels are square slips with a 5u crimson edge on the thread side. Threads are straight 6-unit crimson lines with round caps. The Dakar years are 3px-bordered ink stamps (4px radius) tilted -4deg and +3deg alternately, multiplied onto the paper.

## Components

### Kickstart lever (primary control)
A cut-paper kick-start lever plate (`assets/plates/kick-lever.png`: splined pivot boss with a pinch bolt, dog-leg kraft arm, folding foot peg in ribbed crimson card) on the crank box. The box never moves except to jolt; the lever turns about a pivot at 17% / 50% of its box, and so does its hit area.
- **Rest and kick:** it rests at -15deg (clockwise from pointing right). Dragging (mouse or touch, `touch-action: none`, pointer capture) turns it by the pointer's angle about the pivot. Past the catch at 56deg it resists (movement x 0.45) up to 74deg.
- **Release:** released past the catch it kicks and springs back with back.out(2.4) over 0.55s. Released before the catch it springs back with back.out(1.6) over 0.4s; if it moved more than 6deg the box rattles (about 0.2s, 2px) and nothing else happens.
- **Keyboard:** Enter, Space or ArrowDown on the focused lever swings it to 70deg (0.14s, power3.in), springs it back and kicks. The accessible name says what the kick will do.
- **Hint:** until the first kick or tab, the lever dips 16deg (0.32s power2.out, back 0.5s back.out(2)) every ~2.4s after a 1.4s delay. A dashed crimson arc (stroke 4, dash 10/9) with an arrowhead traces the sweep from rest to the catch, with the stencil "Kick" label, "Kick to rebuild" at step 10. After the first kick they step back to 40% and 75% (400ms).
- **Cursor:** grab, grabbing while dragged.

### Rail tabs (navigation)
Paper tabs pulled from behind the rail board; they jump straight to any step.
- **Shape:** square left edge, 12u radius on the right.
- **Rest:** Tab Kraft over the kraft tile (soft-light), dark brown ink text in Roboto Condensed 700 uppercase, "01 FAIRING".
- **Current step:** crimson with Crimson Blush text and a slight shadow ramp from the left, pulled out further left (`aria-current="step"`).
- **Done:** text at 55% ink.
- **Hover (fine pointers only):** slides 10u left, 220ms ease-out.
- **Jump:** the bike flips through the stages in between at 65ms per stage, then the target step arrives as a kick would.
- **Phones:** a horizontal scroll strip of 10px-radius pills, 14px text, the current tab kept in view.

### The kick sequence (signature motion)
One GSAP timeline per step change writes a small state object; one ticker draws everything from it, and runs only while the hero is on screen.
- **Jolt:** the bike jolts (down 1.4% and 0.6deg, up 0.7%, settle; about 0.37s) and the crank box shakes sideways 3px (0.24s).
- **Leave:** the resting piece flies on along its own path to 2.5x its reach and fades (0.36s, power2.in), while its threads and name strip fade out (0.14s, power1.in) and the sway stops (0.2s).
- **Swap:** the next stage replaces the base image with the next pieces sitting on it exactly (after the stage flips, or 40ms). Two banks of piece layers alternate, so the leaving and arriving pieces move together.
- **Lift:** the piece lifts on its strut with back.out(1.25) over 0.8s. The name strip fades in 0.5s after the swap (0.3s, power2.out); threads draw out over 0.9s from 0.55s, each staggered, labels appearing as each thread reaches them.
- **Sway:** from 0.8s after the swap the lifted piece sways gently on its strut (continuous sine: about 0.45% vertical and 0.5deg), easing in over 0.8s.
- **Rebuild:** a kick at step 10 flips back through every stage to stage 00.

### Lift layers, struts and brads
Each step lifts one to three cut-paper pieces on top of the stage image. A piece translates and rotates by its own vector about its own origin; a folded kraft strut runs from the brass brad where it was fastened to the same point on the lifted piece, appearing as the lift gets under way. Wheels roll on an axle pin instead, turning by the distance they travel, and need no strut. An occluder image keeps the swingarm and fork in front of the wheels as they leave. The crimson backing card shows at the piece's edge in the first 15% of the lift.

### Pointers

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:
- **Do** size everything inside the hero in comp units (`calc(N * var(--u))`) and place it from the comp-measured region variables; never mix in rem or px there, except in the under-900px restack.
- **Do** use the shared ease-out `cubic-bezier(0.23, 1, 0.32, 1)` for every CSS transition and settle; the GSAP strip-down keeps its own named eases (back.out for springs and lifts, power2.in for leaving).
- **Do** make new surfaces out of workbench paper: kraft or pulp paper, notches, tape, brads, thread, punched holes, with the umber two-layer shadow.
- **Do** keep the part name, step number and facts in live text; the strip-down images never carry information on their own.
- **Do** run continuous motion only while it is on screen, and ship a reduced-motion path for any motion: every duration goes to 0, so kicks and tab jumps apply instantly with labels shown, with no jolt, sway or hint, and the settle is removed.
- **Do** embed provenance in every generated raster and write a `.json` sidecar beside every webp.

### Don't:
- **Don't** spend crimson on panels, section backgrounds or decoration; it marks the current step, its threads, the one live instruction, or an ink stamp.
- **Don't** use black, grey or coloured shadows; use Umber Shadow or Contact Umber at an alpha.
- **Don't** set button text, data labels, pointer labels or body copy in the stencil face.
- **Don't** introduce a dark studio ground, glowing callouts or glossy official-ad imagery, and never a Honda logo.
- **Don't** retune the hero title toward the comp's lettering; the stencil face is an accepted deviation.
- **Don't** tie the strip-down to scroll again; scrolling only moves the page.
