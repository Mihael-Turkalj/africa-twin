## 1. Identity and timeline

- **RD03 XRV650 (1988–89):**
  - 647 cc, launched 20 May 1988 [16].
  - Built by HRC [16][17][13].
  - 24 L tank, single front disc, 57 PS [4][23].
  - Not officially sold in the UK [16].
- **RD04 XRV750 (launched Dec 1989 as the 1990 model; 1990–92):**
  - 742 cc, larger carburettors, new oil cooler [3][5].
  - Twin 276 mm front discs; rear disc grew from 240 to 256 mm [3].
  - Upper fairing restyled "in the NXR750 image" [3].
  - Brighter headlights (35/35 W to 60/55 W) and a bigger battery (12 to 14 Ah) [3].
  - JDM figures: 880 mm seat, 209 kg dry / 236 kg kerb, 24 L tank, 130/90-17 rear tyre [3].
  - Tripmaster trip computer added in 1992 [8][17].
  - No longer HRC-built [16].
- **RD07 (1993–95, model letters P–S) [17]:**
  - New square-section semi-double-cradle frame, lighter with a lower centre of gravity [1][6].
  - New fairing, tank (23 L), side covers and rear fender [1].
  - Airbox moved to the front of the tank [1].
  - Lower seat, first radial rear tyre (140/80R17) [1][10].
  - Wheelbase: 1,555 mm JDM [1]; 1,565 mm in European tables [10][17].
- **RD07A (1996 onward, "T" models):**
  - New upper cowl and screen, thicker seat, larger silencer [2][12].
  - Transistor ignition replaced CDI [2].
  - Rack holds a U-lock [2].
  - Improved clutch [12][17].
  - Fork air-assist and shock compression adjuster deleted [10][18].
- **End of production (sources disagree):** Honda says "until 2000" [7], with the 2000 bike the final 750 [6]; Honda Tech Views says 2003 [8]; Wikipedia says production ceased in 2000 and stock was registered until 2003 [17]; ABR says 2001 [15]. **Safe wording:** production ended around 2000–01; new bikes were sold in Europe until 2003.
- **Where built:** Japan. VINs start JH2 [27], and ABR's test bike "rolled off the production line in Japan" [15]. The specific factory is UNVERIFIED; there is no evidence of Italian production.
- **Numbers:**
  - 72,091 Africa Twins (650 and 750) sold in Europe, 20,823 of them in Germany [11]; ABR says about 73,000 [15].
  - MOTORRAD gives about 1,500 RD03, about 4,200 RD04 and over 15,000 RD07, probably German-market figures [10].
  - Japan sold only limited batches: 500 (1990), 300 (1993), 200 (1996) [3][1][2].
- **Why it was dropped:**
  - Falling sales; Honda said the Transalp and Varadero covered the segment [11].
  - MOTORRAD linked the slumps to BMW's GS launches in 1993 and 1999 [11].
  - Visordown: it was "outgunned" by larger rivals [16].
  - A rumoured XRV850 successor never appeared [10][18].

## 2. Rally heritage

- In October 1984 Honda ordered HRC to build a Paris–Dakar winner [9].
- The NXR750 was a liquid-cooled four-stroke OHC V-twin (779–780 cc [18][23]). Its power and V-angle are UNVERIFIED.
- **Paris–Dakar wins:** 1986 Cyril Neveu (Gilles Lalay second), 1987 Neveu, 1988 Edi Orioli, 1989 Gilles Lalay. Four straight wins, 1986–89 [9][5].
- The XRV650 won the Marathon (production) class in 1989 and 1990 [5][9].
- Honda calls the road bike a "racer replica": V-twin, big tank, twin headlights in a large fairing, aluminium bash plate [8]. The engine is **not** the NXR's; it is Transalp/VT500-derived [18][19][12].
- **Livery:** the NXR raced in title-sponsor (Rothmans) colours [9]. The white/red/blue tricolour is HRC's colours, first used on the XRV650 [16][10].

## 3. Assemblies

**Engine**
- 742 cc, liquid-cooled 52° V-twin; bore × stroke 81 × 72 mm; compression 9.0:1 [1][10][17].
- One chain-driven overhead cam per head. Three valves per cylinder (2 intake, 1 exhaust), rocker-operated. Two spark plugs per cylinder [10][8][21].
- Offset dual-pin crankshaft "for smooth operation" [8]. The 76° offset figure is UNVERIFIED for the XRV750.
- Two Keihin CV carburettors, Ø36 mm [10]; motorcyclespecs says 38 mm [20].
- Power:
  - Europe: 44 kW (60 PS) at 7,500 rpm and 62 Nm at 6,000 rpm [10]; alternative figure 45.3 kW / 62.7 Nm [17][20].
  - JDM: 57 PS (1993) rising to 58 PS (1996), and 6.1 kgm at 6,000 rpm [1][2].
  - MCN measured about 52 bhp at the rear wheel [12].
- 5-speed gearbox, wet multi-plate clutch, O-ring chain; oil cooler fitted since 1990 [10][1][3].

**Cooling**
- Twin radiators (left and right), a single electric fan, and a separate oil cooler [21][18].
- The 1993 fairing has vents that guide air around the engine [1].
- Coolant capacity 2.03 L [20].

**Frame and chassis**
- Steel box-section semi-double cradle, redesigned in 1993 [1][6]; Wikipedia: "single downtube with double-loop cradle" [17].
- Aluminium swingarm [10], shortened in 1993 to lower the seat [18].
- Rake/trail: 27°30′ / 108 mm (Honda JDM) [1]; MOTORRAD 27.5° / 112 mm [10].
- Ground clearance 195 mm [1].
- Weight: 207 kg dry / 234 kg kerb (Honda JDM, 1993 and 1996) [1][2]; 236 kg fully fuelled (MOTORRAD) [10].

**Front suspension**
- 43 mm conventional telescopic fork, 220 mm travel [10][15][17]; Showa per Visordown [16].
- Air-assisted 1993–95 [1][22]; air assist dropped in 1996 [10][21]. Owners are advised not to exceed about 0.5 bar [13].

**Rear suspension**
- Pro-Link linkage with a single shock and reservoir [1][21][22].
- Adjustable preload and compression 1993–95; preload only from 1996 [1][10][18].
- Travel: 214 mm in European tables [10][15][17]; Honda JDM gives 220 mm, up from 210 mm [1].

**Wheels and tyres**
- 21-inch front / 17-inch rear spoked wheels with aluminium rims (1.85×21, 2.75×17) [17].
- Tyres: 90/90-21 front and 140/80R17 rear radial from 1993 (the RD04 had 130/90-17) [1][3][10].

**Brakes**
- Front: twin 276 mm discs with twin-piston calipers [10][15][20]; Nissin per MOTORRAD [10].
- Rear: 256 mm disc, single-piston caliper [3][10].

**Fuel system and tank**
- 23 L tank (24 L on the RD03/RD04) with about 5 L reserve [10]; recessed filler cap from 1993 [10].
- The tank sits lower than the carburettors, so an electric fuel pump is needed [16][21].
- The airbox in the front of the tank grew from 5.4 to 7.0 L [1].
- Range: 190 miles at 38 mpg (MCN) [12]; about 200 miles (BikeSocial) [13].

**Bodywork and fairing**
- Twin headlights in a large fairing on a steel front subframe [12][17].
- New upper cowl and screen in 1996 to cut wind on the rider [2].
- Aluminium bash plate, aluminium grab rail, plastic-coated rack [17][14].
- 4 L storage under the seat [1].
- JDM colours: Shasta White tricolour (1993) [1], Sparkling Red (1996) [2], Boon Silver Metallic (1997), Sahara Blue Metallic (1998–99) [26].

**Exhaust**
- Two-into-one: a front header and a separate rear-cylinder pipe feeding one silencer [21]. Silencer side UNVERIFIED (check photos).
- 1993 silencer: 1.5 L more volume but lighter [10]; larger again in 1996 [2]. Material UNVERIFIED; BikeSocial says steel and prone to rot [13].

**Electrics and cockpit**
- Speedometer, tachometer and temperature gauge, plus the Tripmaster LCD. No fuel gauge [21][17].
- Tripmaster: dual odometers, trip countdown and stopwatch [8].
- Alternator 360 W at 5,000 rpm; 12 V 14 Ah battery [10][20][3].
- Ignition: CDI 1993–95, transistor from 1996 [1][2].

**Seat and ergonomics**
- Seat height: 865 mm JDM / 860 mm European (RD07); 870 mm (RD07A) [1][10][12][2][17].
- Seat foam thickened from 75 to 95 mm in 1993 [1].
- Wide bars and a narrow front tyre give quick steering [12]; the seat is tiring on long days [12][13][14]. MOTORRAD calls the RD07 the best-handling Africa Twin [10].

## 4. Strengths and common issues

- **Strengths:** very durable engine, with six-figure mileages common [12][14][10]; easy to ride and capable off-road [13][15].
- **Common issues:**
  - Regulator/rectifier failures that can boil the battery; owners fit MOSFET units [13][14][12].
  - The fuel pump is the best-known weak point, sometimes failing at 30–40k miles [10][14].
  - CDI trouble [13].
  - Sticking chokes [13].
  - Corrosion: fairing subframe, frame, rims, exhaust, and the tank if the bike is stored [12][13][14][10].
  - Brakes feel spongy by modern standards [13][14].

## 5. Did you know

- The 2016 CRF1000L weighed 232 kg, 2 kg less than the 2000 Africa Twin [7].
- The 1996 rack was designed to carry a U-lock [2].
- The XRV650's tank needed four steel sheets instead of three, plus a fuel pump [16].
- MOTORRAD measured 180 km/h top speed and 0–100 km/h in 4.6 s [10].
- The NXR750 was nicknamed the "Queen of Africa" [10][18].
- A new RD07 cost £5,695 in the UK in 1998 [14].

## 6. Name status

"Africa Twin" was revived by Honda for the CRF1000L (EICMA prototype Nov 2014; launched Dec 2015 in Europe and Feb 2016 in Japan) and continues as the CRF1100L (since 2020) [7][24][16][25]. The site must say it is unofficial, and that Honda and Africa Twin are trademarks of Honda Motor Co., Ltd.
